Sunscreen for iPhone

0% blue light on your iPhone from sunset, and darker than the lowest brightness. Automatically, every evening.

Stronger than Night Shift · uses iPhone's own built-in settings, no app · about 3 minutes, once

Why it's stronger than Night Shift

Even at its warmest, Night Shift lets a fair amount of blue light through. iPhone has a hidden accessibility setting, a colour tint, that removes it completely, plus one that dims below the lowest brightness. Apple doesn't let apps change the whole screen, so this setup uses those built-in settings and switches them with the sun.

Set it up

Three short steps. Do them once and it runs by itself every day.

Choose the evening look

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size.
  2. Tap Color Filters, switch it on and choose Color Tint.
  3. Drag Intensity all the way right, and Hue until the screen looks deep orange.
  4. Now switch Color Filters off again. Step 2 turns it on at sunset.

Make it automatic

  1. Open the Shortcuts app and tap Automation at the bottom, then + (New Automation).
  2. Choose Time of Day, pick Sunset, select Run Immediately, tap Next.
  3. Tap New Blank Automation, then Add Action. Search Color Filters and add Set Color Filters (turn on). Tap Done.
  4. Do the same again with Sunrise, and set the action to turn off.

Pause with a triple-click

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Accessibility Shortcut (at the bottom).
  2. Tick Color Filters.
  3. Now a triple-click on the side button switches the evening colour off or on. Handy for a photo or a video.

Good to know

  • Too orange, or not orange enough? Change the Color Tint sliders in Settings. The automations keep working.
  • Screenshots still come out in normal colours. The tint is only on your screen.
  • Even stronger: add Set Brightness to the sunset automation (for example 20%).
  • On a Mac? Sunscreen for Mac does all of this by itself, with no setup.
